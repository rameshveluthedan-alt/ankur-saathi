# How the skills relate to NCF-FS (2022)

**Status: proposed by Claude, not yet confirmed by an educator.** Every judgement below ("covered", "partly", "not covered") is a proposal for the education lead to agree or correct. Nothing in the running app depends on this document.

## Why this exists
Ankur Saathi's 101 skills are aligned to **NIPUN Bharat** (the coded learning outcomes of its Annexure I), the framework of the national foundational literacy and numeracy mission. **NCF-FS** (the National Curriculum Framework for the Foundational Stage, 2022) is a different framework with a different span and a different pitch. This page sets the two side by side so that a reader can see where they agree and where we follow NIPUN.

Two facts about our own NIPUN mapping, for honesty: it covers all 101 skills, but **every one is marked `ai_proposed` and none is yet confirmed by an educator** (column `nipun_review_status`). By match type: 32 exact, 7 finer than a NIPUN outcome, 58 related, 4 with no NIPUN counterpart.

## Method
The NCF-FS learning-outcomes annexure was read from a 31-page PDF of the table (Aims, Curricular Goal, Competency, outcome code, then one column each for Balvatika 1, 2, 3 and Grades 1, 2). It has **128 learning-outcome rows (numbered 1 to 128 with no gap), 13 curricular goals (CG-1 to CG-13) and 68 competencies.** Only the goals that concern foundational literacy and numeracy were compared: **CG-8** (mathematics), **CG-9** (oral language), **CG-10** (reading and writing in Language 1) and **CG-11** (Language 2): 31 competencies in all. Our side is the list of 101 skills (53 numeracy, 48 Hindi literacy). Judgements were made from the skill names and from the outcome text of each stage; they were not tested against children's work.

## 1. Span: where the two frameworks end
NCF-FS covers ages 3 to 8: Balvatika 1 to 3, then Grades 1 and 2. **It stops at Grade 2.** Our 27 Grade 3 skills (16 numeracy, 11 literacy) therefore have no NCF-FS counterpart; they come from NIPUN Bharat, whose goal is attainment by the end of Grade 3. In the other direction, NCF-FS has three Balvatika years where we have one Balvatika level (18 skills).

## 4. Differences of pitch (not gaps)
- **Number range.** NCF-FS reads and writes numerals up to 99 by the end of Grade 2. Our N2.2.1 (3-digit numbers) is placed in Grade 2, following NIPUN Bharat ("numbers up to 999" in Class 2); N3.2.1 (up to 9,999) is Grade 3, also following NIPUN. A state that follows NCF-FS strictly would place these a year later.
- **Division.** NIPUN has equal sharing in Class 2 and grouping in Class 3; NCF-FS starts sharing in Balvatika 2. Our N3.3.5 is at Grade 3.
- **Where our skills go beyond NCF-FS:** 3-digit and 4-digit work, multiplication tables, perimeter and angles, data (pictographs, bar graphs), symmetry. These come from NIPUN Bharat's Grade 3 and later outcomes.

## 5. What this means
- **Nothing changes in the frozen build.** The skills follow NIPUN Bharat on purpose.
- **Roadmap candidates**, in the order I would take them: skills for counting backwards and for equal sharing in Balvatika to Grade 2 (the clearest gaps, both named in both frameworks); informal weight and volume; vocabulary and rhymes in oral language; then an `ncf_codes` column beside `nipun_codes` so the alignment is data, not a document.
- **A lead to check, not a finding:** a secondary summary of the NCF-FS main text found online describes three attainment levels (Beginner, Progressing, Proficient), like our three groups. The annexure used here does not contain them, so it is not verified.
