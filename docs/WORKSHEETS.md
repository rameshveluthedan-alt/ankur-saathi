# Worksheets: the place-value grid and the board-work line

## The carry strip (24 Oct)
A Progressor grid with regrouping has a yellow **"हासिल" carry strip above the TENS column** (`show_carry_box`, set by the code for the Progressors only; the Beginners' sheets have none). Problems 1-3 of a Progressor sheet have the grid; the scaffold fades (4-6 have none), and the You Do tells the children to write the carry separately above.

Asked on the click-through: *the grids seem to have an extra row.* The strip had an **empty box above the ones column** (`.pv-cell-carry-gap`), which no digit ever goes in and which made the grid look like it had an extra row. **Now (option B)** the other columns' headers grow by exactly the strip's height (`.pv-header.pv-header-tall`: the strip is 16 px, the header gets 8 px more padding above and below the label), so the strip reads as a tab under the tens header, and the ones column has three body rows (number, number, answer). Do not give the strip and the headers different heights: the rows would fall out of line.

**A bug fixed by the same change:** the 3-digit grids (a hundreds column) had no spacer at all, so with the carry strip the hundreds column sat 15 px higher than the tens and ones columns. Measured in a real browser: before, every 3-column grid with the strip was misaligned; after, all 26 grids on the recorded sheets (22 two-column, 4 three-column) line up, as does a 3-digit variant with the strip.
**Not done (an idea for later):** a 3-digit sum can also carry from tens to hundreds; the sheet has a carry box above the tens column only.

## The board-work line (24 Oct)
There is no separate "board work" section in a plan. The six problems a teacher copies to the board are **problems 1..required_count of the group's worksheet** (`plan.worksheets[group].problems`). The model refers to them only BY NUMBER (the system may replace a problem's numbers after the model writes), so the plan text could not list them: the You Do said "बोर्ड से सवाल 1 से 6" without saying where they are.
Now the Yojana screen draws, under the You Do of a teach block, **"📝 बोर्ड पर लिखें (सवाल 1-6)"** with the problems numbered like the sheet (`BoardWork.jsx`, `services/boardWork.js`). It is built from the plan's own sheet, so it can never disagree with it, needs no regeneration for existing plans, and appears **only for numeracy** and **only when every required problem is a short sum** (a word problem or a literacy question is left to the sheet; showing nothing is better than showing something wrong). The sheets are still opened from the Yojana tab: **📝 वर्कशीट (Worksheets)**, one button per group.
Tests: `pwa/tests/board_work.test.mjs` (11 checks).

**Copying the line (24 Oct):** the problems were inline spans, so copied as text they ran together ("28 + 352. 46 + 27": the number 352 appears). Each problem is now a block of its own in a three-column grid (two rows of three), so a copy keeps them on separate lines; checked in a real browser (the page's text has one problem per line) and in `board_work.test.mjs` (13 checks).

## The re-teach block's You Do, and repeated sums (24 Oct)
Found on the second real block: the You Do wrote **three sums of its own** (1) 26 + 38, 2) 45 + 27, 3) 19 + 54) AND sent the group to the worksheet's problems 1 to 6: nine problems for a 9-minute You Do, with two numberings that collide. The cause was the rule `you_do_hi: a short try on their own with new numbers`. `RETEACH_MAIN_RULES` now says: **the You Do writes NO problems of its own; the worksheet IS the group's try**; it sends the group to the worksheet problems by NUMBER ('वर्कशीट के सवाल 1 से 6 पहले हल करें') and says where the teacher visits first. The code owns those numbers and shows them under the You Do (the board line above). The same block also used **47 + 36 in the I Do and 36 + 47 as the We Do's "new example"** (the same sum, answer 83, and 83 three times in the lesson): the rules now say **never repeat a sum (47 + 36 and 36 + 47 are the SAME sum) and, as far as possible, give each part a different answer**. These two are PROMPT rules: they are tested for being present (`test_cfu_answers.py`), but whether the model follows them can only be seen in a real Generate: look at the You Do (no list of sums, only the worksheet's) and at the sums of the I Do and We Do (no repeat).

## A two-digit sheet stays two-digit (24 Oct)
`plan_service.repair_worksheet_numbers` replaces plain `a + b` worksheet problems IN CODE (the answer is right by construction; word problems are never touched). It had three triggers (the problem copies an example solved in the lesson; it repeats an answer among the required problems; it is too big for the Beginners' sticks) and, until now, **no tests**. **A fourth trigger:** on a Beginner or Progressor sheet, a problem of two two-digit numbers whose TOTAL is 100 or more is replaced (log: `progressor problem 6: 79 + 21 = ___ -> 39 + 51 = ___ (its total is 100: three-digit numbers are a later (the Proficients') skill)`). The generator already kept its replacements two-digit (a Progressor's: one carry, total 99 or less; a Beginner's: no carry). **A Proficient sheet is not touched** (3-digit sums are its skill; a 2-digit sum over 99 there is left as the model wrote it), nor is a problem with a three-digit addend. Checked on 400 random two-digit sums that reach 100 or more: every replacement is two-digit, needs a carry and has the right answer. The prompt says it too: `orchestrate.range_note` puts one sentence under the skill's number range ("every number you write for it, and the TOTAL of every addition, is at most 99; a two-digit skill never reaches 100"), and the worksheet rules repeat it. Tests: `test_two_digit_limit.py`.

## The re-teach block, third round of prompt rules (24 Oct)
After the second real block: (1) **the I Do uses the SAME sum for the wrong and the right working** (the "never repeat a sum" rule I had added made the model use two different sums, which lost the contrast: it is now the one exception, and the `i_do_hi` rule says "do the SAME sum again correctly"); (2) **the show-me must not use a result the lesson has already written on the board** (CFU rules; the code only warns, see CFU.md); (3) **for numeracy, `other_groups_hi` and every independent activity must NAME the practice** (two or three example problems at that group's level, obeying its number range): the Proficients' line had said "problems written on the board" without any problem. These are PROMPT rules: they are tested for being present, and whether the model follows them can only be seen in a real Generate.
