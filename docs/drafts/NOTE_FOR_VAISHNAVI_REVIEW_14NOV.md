## 1. The new L2.7.2 plan — `docs/batch/review/L2.7.2.html` (about 15 minutes)

**What it is.** The whole plan, in the order a teacher meets it. Once you sign it, teachers see this plan whenever the AI limit is reached, instead of a fresh one, so please read it as a plan any teacher could use as it is. Open the file in a browser and read top to bottom.

**Please check:**
1. **Hindi** — natural and respectful to the children; the teacher's first person neutral ("सुनता/सुनती हूँ"); nothing awkward or word-for-word.
2. **Beginners' dictation (L2.7.1)** — are the 5 sentences right for Class 2, and do they practise the matras of the skill (छोटी-बड़ी मात्रा, ं / ँ, है / हैं)? The sentences should appear only in the teacher's part, never on the child's sheet or the board.
3. **Progressors' and Proficients' writing (L2.7.2, L3.7.1)** — are the topics familiar to children, and is the checklist fair for the grade and something a teacher can *see* (for example "कम से कम 3 वाक्य", "हर वाक्य के अंत में ।")?
4. **The short items on each sheet** (joining sentences, full stops, putting sentences in order) — is each answer key correct, with one right answer?
5. **Checks for understanding** — they now aim at your nine mistakes (for example "दीदी / दिदी"). Are the questions clear, and is the "wrong answer to listen for" realistic?
6. **The lesson** — do I Do, We Do and You Do flow sensibly for each group, and does the You Do send the children to their sheet's task (not to the teacher's own example topic)?

**How to answer:** "OK", or a short note per point. If it is OK, we sign it in your name and it replaces the old L2.7.2 plan.

## 2. Tasks for skills checked by watching — `docs/drafts/OBSERVED_TASKS_FOR_REVIEW.csv`

**Why.** Almost half the skills (48) are checked by the teacher watching the children, but their plans had written worksheets, so there was nothing to watch. Each such group now gets a hands-on or spoken task, the materials for it (a kit item if the class has one, otherwise everyday objects: leaves, pebbles, buttons, bottle caps), and what A / PA / NA look like for that task. Most schools cannot print, so the back-up worksheet can be drawn on the board.

**The file:** 9 rows, one per group, from three plans — N0.1.1 (sorting by one attribute), N1.4.1 (comparing lengths) and L2.3.1 (reading conjunct words); some groups are on a neighbouring skill (N2.4.1, L1.4.2, L2.3.2). Each row: the task, kit item, everyday objects, A / PA / NA, and the You Do as written.

**Please check, for each row:**
1. **The task** — does it really show the skill? Can a group of 6-12 children do it in pairs in about 5-6 minutes while the teacher watches the children the app names?
2. **The materials** — can a typical class collect them? Is the kit item one a classroom would have?
3. **A / PA / NA** — are the three levels clearly *different*, and *visible* to someone watching? Are the cut-offs right for the grade?
4. **The You Do** — does it say exactly what to hand out and what the children do? (Three rows still say only "चीज़ें"; we have asked the AI to name the objects in future.)
5. Anything unsafe or impractical in a classroom.

**How to answer:** fill in the last two columns ("Vaishnavi: OK / change", "Vaishnavi: comment") and send the file back, as you did for the mistakes. Your answers also tell us whether the rules are right for all 48 such skills: if you would describe A / PA / NA differently, we will change the rule for every one of them.
